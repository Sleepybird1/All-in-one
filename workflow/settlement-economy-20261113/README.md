## Repeatable orders

- The Regional Economy icon shows the village's simulated work crew, food and fuel reserves, and active road trading partners. Its goods stay in the village simulation. Player contracts still require real deliveries.
- Small farms and timber crews trade through verified dry starter roads. Five recent consecutive service days and complementary needs can qualify bordering villages to found or join a nation automatically. Distant or disconnected villages stay independent until their land connection qualifies.
- Villages can spend eight verified oak planks on one adjoining dry road cell per day. Existing owners and severe known hazards can block the route. This changes the economic border without placing or replacing player blocks.
- MapFrontiers publishes new alliances automatically. Open JourneyMap and right-click a managed border for Nation Information, including exact population, stability, integrity, district count, and capital.
- Discovered ruined portals, outposts, and dungeon starts can reduce local simulated output. Native structure evidence is recorded when a chunk loads, and the economy applies the effect during its scheduled work.

- An NPC introduces the first order and its customer. The village dashboard lists stock, shortfalls, recent deliveries, reputation, available production, and each order's destination. The first delivery is manual. Belts, hoppers, Create logistics and a ComputerCraft peripheral can automate acceptance, dispatch, delivery, and payment once unlocked.
- Each settlement has a finite treasury and inventory. It consumes food, fuel and service parts, and keeps reserves for local production. Villages make goods with actual workers or equipment and earn funds through trade and services. Operating demand rotates with stock, installed facilities, imports, outages, and diplomacy. An accepted contract locks its quantity, price, and destination.
- Standing orders recur indefinitely for operating supplies, maintenance and replacement machines. Optional growth projects take larger batches of pumps, compressors, motors, breakers, transformers, control assemblies, and structural stock. A completed project adds working capacity, which creates different orders. Higher tiers use larger batches, higher power, more suppliers, and more complex machines. Keep ordinary recurring orders affordable after a major project.
- Call villages with an established route **trade partners**. Registering new partners unlocks destinations, suppliers, and more regional order capacity. Qualifying for large orders also requires recent repeat service across existing partners. A fresh village has a small initial order book, and old partners can keep their standing orders when new ones join.
- Track village-to-player or village-to-team reputation and independent village-to-village relations. Reliable deliveries and training improve terms. Broken commitments, shortages and embargoes can raise landed costs or reduce access. Settlements can offer trusted suppliers a premium on work and lower prices when selling to them. A confirmed delivery changes future terms. Politics can affect route availability, tolls and escort demand before it changes anything in the world.
- Nearby villages can form and split trade leagues. Members and treaty partners can get priority on future allocations, and leagues can pool funded orders when trading with other leagues. See [Trade leagues and rival blocs](CONTRACT_ECONOMY.md#trade-leagues-and-rival-blocs).
- Established leagues can become nations with millions of simulated residents. Loaded villages have a provisional visible population of 30–40, and national demand drives real production and freight. See [Nations and population](CONTRACT_ECONOMY.md#nations-and-population).
- The proposed nation simulation draws borders from settlement routes and shows them in JourneyMap through MapFrontiers. Territorial surveys inform resource estimates, and sustained shortages with failed relief can lead to secession. See [Borders and territorial resources](CONTRACT_ECONOMY.md#borders-and-territorial-resources).
- FTB Quests can observe milestones such as first delivery, three recurring orders with two partners, first powered district, and first interplanetary route. Count qualifying completed orders once, using the contract system's confirmed receipts. Standing orders continue after the milestones and Part One credits.
- Solo suppliers and FTB Teams can compete for optional fixed-volume tenders with a capped award per lot. Team members can manufacture, haul, or deliver on the same contract with one shared payout and standing. Keep core progression orders available to every eligible team. Bind accepted terms and receipts to the account that accepted them, use explicit shipment permissions, and guard against team switching or repeat claims.

## Physical freight

A producer puts output into a depot inventory and a native Create Packager withdraws stock into addressed packages. A player collects the first boxes from a counter or depot. Later, Frogports and trains move them to another settlement, and ship docks carry pallets, sealed packages or fluid tanks between worlds. A package has a unique shipment ID and fixed source and destination. Record cargo custody at pickup and count only the amount actually accepted into the matching destination, once. A rejected box or full dock keeps its cargo available for return or retry. Use the existing Industrial Colonies physical freight evidence as route proof, with a separate committed-inventory receipt for settlement.

NPC example: “The mill packed your replacement pumps. Collect the boxes at the east freight depot and deliver them to the refinery. Bring back the empty railcar and I can book the next load.”

## Certified freight fleets

Build a real ship and run the intended route before commissioning copies. Prove each direction with the planned load and verify docking, cargo, fuel use, and reserves. Test the intended passenger load with seats, oxygen, and life support before extending certification to passengers. Bind the route record to the tested hull, equipment, loading limits, and dock pair. Changes that affect those limits or swap the route require another qualifying run.

### Local cargo pilot

1. Use two owned, linked airship stations in the same dimension. Give the trial ship a linked connector, local linked cargo storage, and a separate Create tank with TFMG kerosene or diesel. Use the matching propulsion fuel supply during the trial. Keep the same cargo in the same slots and tanks, and fly each trial without refueling.
2. At the locked origin, run `/icfleet trial begin <ship> <origin> <destination> <dx> <dy> <dz> <side>`. The coordinates are the fuel access offset from the transponder in the assembled ship's plot. Record a fresh native route during the loaded trial. At the locked destination, run `/icfleet trial finish <ship> <segment>`. Repeat independently in reverse with the intended return manifest.
3. Run `/icfleet certify <outbound-trial> <inbound-trial> <fuel-reserve-mb>`. Build copies with your usual schematic workflow. Each copy needs its own transponder UUID, the tested hull and equipment, an empty owned schedule, and the exact tested cargo for its direction. The pilot checks exact cargo contents because Sable reports hull mass separately from inventory.
4. With both station endpoints loaded and the copy physically locked at the origin, use `/icfleet dispatch <certificate> <ship> <origin>`. The system charges the measured fuel in advance and starts one native trip. Native Aeronautics Logistics supplies offscreen playback and rematerialization. Keep passengers off the automated ship and wait for `/icfleet status <ship>` to say `COMPLETE` before unloading or refueling. Use a separate parking signal to hold the destination connector after the native schedule clears its temporary dock signal.
5. If a run is interrupted, inspect the native status. With the same body physically locked at its certified destination, `/icfleet verify_arrival <ship>` checks the exact manifest and charged fuel balance before closing the trip. At the original locked dock with native automation stopped, `/icfleet cancel_at_origin <ship>` closes the trip without issuing fuel. Settlement receipts still require a separate verified ground transfer.

The pilot reuses native playback and its per-player limit. Industrial Colonies schedules arrival checks with a fixed budget and creates no route chunk tickets. The one-shot permit is limited to the paid launch call. Depot settlement requires a verified native arrival and the exact marked package. Fleet passenger service, schematic manufacturing, unattended dispatch, and measured multi-ship throughput are follow-up work.

A shipyard can then build copies from the schematic. Each commissioned copy consumes the complete bill of materials and gets its own identity, fuel, maintenance budget, and manifest. Estimate its range from verified fuel use at load, tank capacity, reserve, and refueling stops. If the return leg has a different load or requires refueling, record that leg separately. Start service only when the origin has the actual cargo or passenger reservations and enough supplies for the assigned leg.

Run distant trips as scheduled records with a route, travel time, resource costs, and a single authoritative cargo or passenger manifest. On arrival, the receiving dock must accept the actual delivery before settlement pays it. A full dock or interrupted trip holds the shipment for retry or return. Persist progress across reloads and preserve existing cargo, route, and loaded-cell restrictions. Keep player-crewed flights under the normal live flight controls.

Show approaching ships near active ports with their actual schematic appearance and scheduled motion. Start with a visual representation driven by the scheduled trip. Investigate optional physical handoff for boarding, docking, or encounters only after verifying that the same ship and manifest survive approach, departure, and restart. Keep a separate budget for visible and fully simulated ships, and leave distant route chunks unloaded. The goal is a fleet with more than two ships in service and several visible moving freighters where performance permits. Measure the supported fleet and visibility sizes before choosing limits.

The installed Aeronautics Automated Logistics code has unloaded playback and stored-body restoration for local routes. Use one fixed same-dimension route for the first pilot and verify that full and empty cargo loads are reflected in the mass used to set limits. Qualify planetary transfers separately against the existing freight handoff rules.

## Passenger transport

- Settlements request passengers when a verified facility has vacancies, an apprenticeship has an available instructor, or a commissioned offworld habitat has room. A steelworks could request machinists, a rail town could send apprentices to a training center and book their return, and a new Martian habitat could request a settlement crew. A request needs a willing group at its origin and available beds, jobs, or training at its destination.
- Book groups by profession and headcount. Reserve them at the origin, put them on a real passenger manifest when they board, and release them to their destination only after verified arrival. Local trains use passenger stations and seats. Space routes also require a commissioned habitat, passenger capacity, oxygen, and a legal dock. A missed or diverted route holds the group for recovery and pays once after actual arrival.
- Start with one train transfer and one visible group at the platforms. Process routine demand and travel as aggregate records. Instantiate a small number of representative villagers at loaded stops for dialogue and boarding. Keep named NPCs and player-managed villagers opt-in and track their UUIDs only on active trips. Use existing train or ship routing and sample departure and arrival events. A pending passenger order never forces chunks to load.
- Completing a move fills real staffing or housing capacity and can create later commute, return, food, or maintenance orders. Reuse the contract ledger for player or FTB Team payouts and prevent duplicate seats, duplicate arrivals, and credit for missed runs. The design needs verification against the actual native train and spacecraft APIs, especially living passengers during planetary transfer.

## Structure effects and defense design

- Treat nearby discovered structures as inputs to settlement conditions. Confirm the actual generated structure and its relationship to a registered village before applying effects. Track each discovery once and process outcomes through the existing settlement simulation.
- Determine jurisdiction at chunk resolution. Each village has a maximum local territory radius. A nation combines the territories its villages can hold and connect.
- Draw the map outline through chunk corners, allowing diagonal segments for a more natural border. Keep actual territory ownership and route connectivity checked against the underlying chunks.
- A nearby ruined portal can create a bounded Nether raid risk. An unresolved attack can cause simulated deaths, interrupted trade, and lower stability. Clearing, securing, or containing the site can reduce exposure. Keep any live raid participants within the loaded-village performance budget.
- Dungeon types can affect a district's health, safety, production, or trade in different ways. Give each type its own positive and negative effects, with outcomes changing when players clear or secure the site. Show the cause and affected village on the board.
- A structure threat can seize connected chunks and interrupt overland trade. Record missed access as a route and stability problem. A valid alternate sea or air route preserves effective trade. An extended blockade can halt nation formation or contribute to a rare split under the existing secession rules.
- Alliances let villages and nations share warnings, escorts, relief, and defense. A village militia and a national army have separate simulated recruits, trained personnel, reserves, equipment, and technology. Track mobilization, supply, casualties, and recovery in the settlement ledger.
- A village can post weapons and equipment contracts for its own militia. National weapons and technology procurement goes through its capital or a designated logistics hub, with real funds, goods, and verified delivery. Use aggregate military outcomes and reserve limited physical encounters for when players are present.

## Server performance

- Keep compact saved records for partner status, inventories, outstanding orders, funds and recent delivery totals. Recompute settlements on delivery, a relevant block event, a meter sample, or a staggered regional deadline. Run loaded entities through their existing native ticks. Unloaded settlements may update bounded economic bookkeeping, while physical cargo and meter credit require actual evidence.
- Schedule due work with a fixed budget and a fair queue. Index orders, reservations and in-flight quantities by settlement, contract and shipment. Cache dashboard summaries, send only changed pages, and aggregate completion history by period. Keep durable receipt IDs or high-water marks so archive pruning preserves duplicate protection.
- Keep loaded convoy count, route tickets, NPC count, and catch-up steps bounded. Never create route entities for every settlement. Start with one loaded producer and two receivers. Add capacity only after measuring it.
- Compare identical test saves with the same seed and active routes. Record cold startup, world join, p50/p95/p99 MSPT, heap after GC, loaded chunks, ticket count, route count, and work backlog. The pack already has Spark. An initial provisional budget is ≤1 ms added p95 tick work at 20 active depots, within the game's 50 ms tick interval, with stable post-GC heap and no accumulating backlog. Test alongside the real pack before selecting caps.
